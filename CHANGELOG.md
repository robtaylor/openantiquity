# Changelog

All notable changes to OpenAntiquity are recorded here. The format follows
[Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/). OpenAntiquity is a deployed
service, so entries are dated, not versioned.

## [Unreleased]

### What this means for you

OpenAntiquity has been live at openantiquity.org since 12 November 2026. This is what it does for you.

- The code is open. It is published at github.com/robtaylor/openantiquity under the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3, so anyone may read it, change it and run their own copy, and whoever runs a changed copy has to share its source.
- It is called OpenAntiquity, at openantiquity.org. While it was being built it was called Field Club, and two early letters to record holders carry that name.
- You can look at England's LIDAR as slope, sky-view, relief and other views made for spotting earthworks, and light it from any direction you like. You don't need an account to look at anything.
- Designated sites and other heritage records sit on the same map, each with who published it and under what licence. Where a record office doesn't let us keep its records, the map asks it directly or links to its own site.
- A finding can be marked as a building or ruin: the walls or foundations of a house, a barn, a chapel or another building, standing or fallen. It sits beside "Settlement earthworks" in the list of what it looks like.
- You can mark something you think is there. Other people then take a second look without seeing what you said, and the finding climbs from "Spotted" to "Seen by others" and on as people agree and add evidence.
- A heritage record linked to a finding now says what kind of record it is, where it comes from and under what licence, and the start of what its source says. Its name opens the record's page here, and "See the original record" opens the source's own page in another tab.
- A name read from the six-inch map of about 1900 now has "See it on the old map", on its page and wherever it is linked to a finding. It opens that map at the place the lettering sits, on the National Library of Scotland's viewer.
- Zoomed out, the map no longer fills with numbered squares counting heritage records, so the findings stand out. The counts are a switch in the Layers panel, "Counts when zoomed out", under the heritage records, and a link you share keeps it on. Single records still appear as you zoom in.
- Findings stay on the map and in the Explore list however far out you zoom, where they used to vanish below a county's width. Where there are more in view than the map can draw at once, it says so, and zooming in shows the rest.
- A marking you haven't finished no longer hides when you close the tool. It stays on the map, dashed, and pressing it takes you back to it. "Mark a finding" pressed somewhere else leaves the map where it is and asks whether to go to the unfinished one or discard it and start here, where it used to jump to it. A discarded marking can be undone.
- The map zooms in two levels further, so something a couple of metres across can be drawn round without its points crowding each other. The terrain itself is no sharper there: the survey is one-metre data.
- "Change the shape" on something small now brings it up to a size you can work on, where it used to stop while the shape was still a few pixels across.
- A right-click on the map, which on a Mac is also a two-finger click or a Ctrl-click, no longer leaves a drawing for "Open elsewhere". While you are marking or changing a shape it does nothing.
- The map opens on Multi-direction shade, lit from three sides so that nothing hides by running along the light. Composite and the other views are a press away in the Layers panel.
- Zoomed out, the map names its cities and towns again, with smaller places appearing as you go in. The record counts used to crowd them out.
- Opening the Layers panel and closing it no longer takes the map back to the record, finding or investigation whose page is open. The map stays where you had moved it.
- Clicking the map inside a very large record, such as the extent of a county-wide survey, no longer opens that record and carries the map off to its middle. A record that fills the whole view isn't opened by a click inside it, and a record you open by clicking the map leaves the map where you clicked.
- Opening a road you're zoomed in on, or pressing "Change the shape" on it, no longer takes the map off to the middle of the road or out to the whole of it. The map stays on the stretch you were looking at.
- A marking you haven't saved is no longer lost when you open the Layers panel, a record or anything else over it, or close the marking panel. Open "Mark a finding" again and it carries on where you left it.
- Jumping to somewhere nobody has looked at yet no longer ends with "the layer isn't loading". The terrain there takes a few seconds to draw the first time, and the map now waits for it.
- The switch for the roads and names drawn over the terrain is the first thing in the Layers panel. Switch it off to see the terrain alone.
- A finding can be a spot, a line or a shape. Tap once for a spot, keep tapping for a line, straight or curved through your points, and tap the first point to close it into a shape. A line can run as far as a Roman road, and you can carry it on from either end later. On a phone a long press on a point switches it between straight and curved, and every gesture has a button that does the same.
- While you draw, a short list on the map, where the search box usually sits, shows what each tap, drag and key does at that moment, with a button to search instead and one to fold it away. A faint dot half way along each stretch of a line adds a point there when you drag it, which is how you bend a curve where it strays.
- When you add to a line others have already looked at, their second looks still count, and the page says how much is new and asks for a look at it. Most of what they looked at has to stay where it was.
- Where findings overlap, a tap opens the most specific one, a point before a line and a line before an area's edge. Where two are alike, you choose from a short list. An area opens from its edge, so whatever lies inside it stays easy to reach.
- A protected line or shape is shown to people who aren't signed in only as its one-kilometre square, on the map, on its page, in the activity feed and in downloads.
- While you take a second look, the heritage records are hidden as well as the findings, so a record at the spot can't answer the question for you. They come back once you've answered.
- You can try marking, answering and scanning before you join. What you do is kept in your browser, and when you sign in from the same tab it waits for you to press send, so nothing someone else left in an open tab is saved as yours.
- On a shared computer, nothing the last person drafted is saved to your account or shown as yours. You see that it's there, by kind alone, and can discard it, or save it as yours if you made it.
- An emailed sign-in link opened on another device asks you to type your address, and signs you in only if it's the one the link was sent to.
- You can sign out on every device at once from your account page. A session ends after 90 days unused, and a year after you signed in at most.
- A mention whose link would show where a protected finding is shows its title alone to people who see only the square, and says why. A map view or height profile near such a finding in an investigation says it is held back.
- Signing in is with Google, Apple, Microsoft, Facebook or OpenStreetMap, or an emailed link. You must be 16 or over to add anything.
- Nothing anyone links, writes or draws beside a protected finding gives away more than its one-kilometre square to people who see only the square: map links, saved map views, height profiles and an investigation's notes and area included.
- Nobody can tell how a named person answered a second look from what happens when their role changes or they are banned.
- A map view drawn near a protected finding is held back in every investigation, not only one that holds the finding, and still after the finding is taken off. An investigation's area near one is shown as whole one-kilometre squares, and its title is hidden the same way in the activity feed.
- If you edit an investigation that hides a place from you, what you can't see is kept as it was: edit around each "[position hidden]" and leave it in place.
- Your own map views and notes in an investigation are always shown to you as you made them.
- Positions written in full-width figures, plus codes and short links to map sites are hidden or refused like grid references.
- Once you have signed in with an emailed link, that browser can still ask for a link when strangers have asked for too many for your address.
- Names that pass for staff or for a deleted account, such as "Site Moderator" or "Former member (deleted)", are refused, and changing your handle again and again can't reserve names.
- Pages from OpenAntiquity make your browser fetch nothing from Google, Facebook or the other sign-in providers: profile pictures from them aren't shown.
- Possible burial mounds and other sites at risk from looting are shown as a one-kilometre square to anyone who isn't signed in.
- A finding is open or protected. The restricted level is gone, because it kept a finding's exact place from people who could still draw beside it.
- There are no private messages. Everything people say about a finding is public and attached to it.
- If you find an object, OpenAntiquity records nothing about it and tells you who to report it to in your part of the UK.
- A second look counts the moment you press your answer. You can then say what you think it is, or not, before you see what the finder and everyone else said. If you leave in between, you're offered the same choice when you come back.
- If a protected finding's title or note says where it is more closely than its one-kilometre square, the form says which one and what to take out.
- When a video, article, podcast or paper talks about a finding, you can add it to the finding's page. You see what the link is before you save it, and a video opens at the moment the finding comes up.
- A finding's page has a timeline of what has happened on the ground and on record, such as visits, surveys, digs and new heritage records, and says which of them moved it up.
- You can keep a notebook for a piece of work, an investigation, gathering findings, records, map views and notes, and publish it when you're ready.
- "Scan a square" sends you to a square few people have looked at, so the whole country gets looked over and not only the famous places.
- Each person has a page showing what they have found and checked. Someone aged 16 or 17 shows only a handle.
- Anything that looks wrong has a Report button. Moderators see reports in one place, and everything a moderator does is logged.
- A ground photo is shown to everyone only after a moderator, archaeologist or trusted member has approved it.
- A guide shows how to read LIDAR, with real earthworks beside the modern things that look like them, and the about page gives the terms, the privacy notice and how to ask for something to be taken down. Those four notices are drafts until they have been checked.

### Changed

For whoever runs the service, as it moves to Fly.io (Decision 0018):

- Buckets are reached through the S3 interface, such as Tigris, and no longer through Cloud Storage. `FIELDCLUB_STORAGE_BACKEND=s3` replaces `gcs`, `FIELDCLUB_S3_BUCKET`, `FIELDCLUB_S3_PREFIX` and `FIELDCLUB_S3_NAMESPACE_BUCKETS` replace the `FIELDCLUB_GCS_*` settings, and terrain folders are written `s3://bucket/prefix`. A `gs://` folder is refused at start-up.
- Anything kept in a bucket needs `AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID`, `AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY` and `AWS_ENDPOINT_URL_S3`, as Fly sets them when a Tigris bucket is attached, and the settings refuse to load without them. `AWS_REGION` is `auto` when unset.
- `DATABASE_URL`, as Fly attaches it, is read on a Fly machine when `FIELDCLUB_DATABASE_URL` is unset, and ignored anywhere else. Either may be a plain `postgres://` address, and Fly's pooled host is replaced by its direct one. The address used is logged with its password masked.
- `FIELDCLUB_CLIENT_IP_HEADER` names a header the proxy sets to the visitor's address. On Fly it must be `Fly-Client-IP`, and production accepts it in place of `FIELDCLUB_TRUSTED_PROXY_HOPS`.
- On a Fly machine the settings refuse to start unless `FIELDCLUB_ENV=prod` and `FIELDCLUB_CLIENT_IP_HEADER` are set, as they refuse anything but production on Cloud Run.
- The image listens on 8080 unless the platform sends another `PORT`, and no longer sets `CPL_MACHINE_IS_GCE`.
- The live event stream finds a database connection that went silent, as one may while Fly has a machine suspended, within about ten seconds of the next visitor, and tells browsers to refresh what they show.
- A full ingest run whose lock went with its database connection fails with `ingest_lock_lost` instead of deleting records.
- The deployment is now Fly.io: `fly.toml` for the web app, a second app for the jobs that alone holds Defra's login, `infra/fly_jobs.py` to build the jobs app's own image and start each job as a machine under a time limit, `infra/fly-secrets.sh` to set secrets without showing them, and `deploy.yml` deploying with a deploy token from main. `infra/README.md` is the new runbook. The Google Cloud deployment stays in `infra/terraform` until the cut-over, and an image built from main no longer runs there.
- `FIELDCLUB_REDIRECT_HOSTS` lists the site's other names. A request for one is answered 308 with the same path on `FIELDCLUB_PUBLIC_URL`.
- `fieldclub-jobs due` deletes old sign-ins, then runs whichever of the channel scan and the source refresh is due, for a scheduler that can only start it every hour.
- `FIELDCLUB_TERRAIN_API_FETCH=false` stops the API adding tiles to the mirror, for a web app that holds no supply login: a tile it lacks is answered `elevation_unavailable` without writing anything.
- Terrain tiles draw on Fly. GDAL was told where the bucket is on one thread only, and tiles are drawn on others, so every tile failed there. The setting now reaches every thread.
- A deploy checks the elevation mirror before it replaces anything (`fieldclub-release`, Decision 0027): every tile of the catalogue is there and one can be read as a map tile reads it. `FIELDCLUB_TERRAIN_CHECK_ALLOW_MISSING` lets a deploy through with tiles missing. A server that can't add tiles says in its log which ones the mirror lacks.
- On Fly a machine draws four tiles at once and keeps 16 waiting, inside its soft limit of 20 requests, so a burst of uncached tiles is answered `tiles_busy` before it can hold every request slot. The jobs app is no longer given a session key.
- The settings refuse a mirror and national overview folder that are the same or one inside the other, and an overview build removes only its own build files.
- A misnamed or missing bucket is now an error at the first read or write, instead of an empty folder or an elevation lock that waits and then says "being prepared".
- `infra/jobs/mirror_shard.py` takes the shares to run as `--shares 4 --share N`, one or more, in place of Cloud Run's task variables.

### Added

- `GET /meta` says whether the development sign-in is on, which age bands the welcome step accepts, the contributor terms, each feature class's starting sensitivity and, per nation, where to report a find.
- `GET /auth/session` answers with the signed-in person or null. `GET /auth/me` carries a stable `id`, and `PATCH /auth/me` removes the picture a sign-in provider gave.
- `POST /findings` takes a `client_ref`, so a draft sent twice is saved once.
- `GET /records/{id}/findings`, the nearest settlement in `GET /geo/point`, what each context link shows, and how to read each live source.
- Explore lists the findings in view beside the map, kept in step with it, with filters for step, state and what a finding looks like. Choosing a finding in the list or on the map opens it and marks it.
- "Mark a finding": place a mark by tapping, clicking or with the keyboard, drag or nudge it, pick what it looks like and save. A title, the view you were using and the period are filled in for you. Signed out, the finding waits in your browser as a draft and is saved once you sign in.
- A finding's page shows how far it has got up the ladder and what would move it up, each with a button for what you could do about it. You can give it a second look there, add evidence of every kind, ground photos included, link a heritage record, join the discussion and follow it. It names the nearest place, and one press shows it as its finder saw it. Its finder can edit it, withdraw it and protect it more, and a moderator can lower its protection with a reason for the log. What anyone is offered is what the server says they may do. Evidence says how many people endorsed and disputed it.
- Second look: findings waiting for another pair of eyes, one at a time, in the view their finder used and with nothing about what they thought. Answer with one press, or the keys 1, 2 and 3, which sends it at once, add detail if you like, and then see the finding, how your answer compares and whether it moved it up. Nearby findings come first. Signed out, your answer waits as a draft you can still change, and is saved and revealed when you sign in.
- Mentions on a finding's page, newest first, with adding one after a look at what the address holds, and removing your own. Mentions the channel scan found are marked as found automatically. "Recent mentions" lists them across the map, with each finding as you may see it.
- The follow-up timeline on a finding's page, drawn from its site visits, surveys, digs, heritage records and designations, in date order.
- Help wanted: the next steps on everyone's findings, by the kind of work they are, such as map detectives checking old maps and sky checkers looking at aerial photos. Each finding opens with the form for that work ready. Second looks are offered only through the blind queue.
- Investigations: a notebook tied to the map for one piece of work. Start one with a title, then gather findings, heritage records, saved map views, height profiles, references and notes in it, in the order you choose, from its own page or with "Add to an investigation" on a finding, a record, a height profile or the layer panel. Notes take headings, bold, italics, lists and links. It starts private; make it unlisted or public to invite people by their handle, who join once they accept. Download it as GeoJSON, as a spreadsheet with Historic Environment Record field names, or as a printable report. Something taken out of one can be put back with “Undo” for a few seconds.
- Scan a square: one press sends you to a one-kilometre square few people have looked at, near where you are looking, framed on the map with the ground outside it shaded. Look it over in any view, mark what you see, then say "Nothing obvious" or "I marked something", or skip it, and one more press brings the next. Saving a finding you marked there brings you back to the square. An empty square counts: it shows the ground was checked. Signed out, your squares wait in your browser and are saved when you sign in. The scan coverage layer shows how many people have looked over each square in view.
- Activity: what people have been doing, newest first, in plain sentences, everywhere or in the map's view and by kind. A second look's part in it names nobody. It keeps up by itself while the page is open.
- Notifications: a quiet count on Account when a finding you follow changes, and the list on your account page, marked read as you see it.
- A finding's page shows a move up the ladder as it happens, without a reload.
- Profiles, a Report button on findings, evidence, comments, photos, mentions, investigations and profiles, the moderators' queue and log, and photo approval.
- The guide to reading LIDAR, the about page with its credits, and four draft notices: contributor terms, privacy, reporting and takedown.
- Downloads are named `openantiquity-…`, an export's id column is `openantiquity_id`, the cookies are `openantiquity_session`, `openantiquity_oauth` and `openantiquity_email_link`, and requests that change data carry `X-OpenAntiquity-Client`.

### Security

- Every page and answer is sent with a Content-Security-Policy, and no other site may show OpenAntiquity in a frame.
- A request body over 1 MB is refused before it is read, except a photo, which may be 15 MB.
- The API's interactive docs and its schema aren't served in production.
- Production refuses a secret key shorter than 32 characters and cookies that aren't Secure.
- The session cookie is `__Host-openantiquity_session`, which another site on a neighbouring address can't set.
- A session ends after 90 days without use and a year after sign-in, and `POST /auth/logout-everywhere` ends all of yours at once.
- Expired sessions, and sign-in links a day after they are spent or expire, are deleted by `fieldclub-jobs purge-sign-ins`, and by every hourly `fieldclub-jobs due` run on Fly.
- An emailed sign-in link opened in another browser signs in only when you type the address it was sent to, and a stranger asking for links to your address can no longer use up your allowance or spend the link you're about to click.
- Handles and display names that pass for OpenAntiquity, its staff or a former member, in look-alike letters or with hyphens, are refused, and a handle someone gives up is held back from others for a year.
- A mention's or an evidence item's link on a protected finding reaches only people who see the finding exactly, and a map link to the spot is refused on such a finding.
- Saved map views and height profiles near a protected finding are held back, and the area is coarsened, in investigations, for people who see the finding only as its square.
- Outlines and areas have at most 2,000 points.
- Map tiles are limited to 2,400 a minute from one address, and one address may hold at most eight live update streams, six on each machine on Fly.
- A shape with an empty or malformed part, such as a polygon with an empty hole, is refused before the geometry library sees it. One request with such an outline used to end the whole API process (a fault in GEOS, reported as [libgeos/geos#1604](https://github.com/libgeos/geos/issues/1604)). A publisher's polygon with an empty hole is stored without it.
- A second look and a vote count at the weight of the role their giver had when they gave it, and keep counting through a ban.
- A page whose server names an odd character set is read as UTF-8, and a page that breaks the reader no longer causes an error.
- Record addresses from publishers are kept only when they are web addresses, and record names and summaries are cut to length.

### Removed

- `avatar_url` from every person in the API, and from `PATCH /auth/me`.
- `findings_reassessed` from the moderators' ban and unban answers.
- `confirm` from `POST /auth/email/verify`, which takes the typed `email` instead.
